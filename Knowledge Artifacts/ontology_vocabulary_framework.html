DECOMPOSITION RATIONALE "Decision Science" as used in the source document is a conflation of a disciplinary umbrella with a set of distinct, non-interchangeable analytical frameworks. Before any of these frameworks can be instantiated as ontology classes or applied to domain engineering, they must be individually defined, scoped, and related. The following is a SKOS-conformant decomposition.
Decision Science Disciplinary Umbrella
The interdisciplinary domain that studies how decisions are made (descriptive), how they should be made (normative), and how decision-making can be improved through formal methods, behavioral understanding, and organizational design. It is the parent class — not a method itself. The following sub-disciplines are its narrower concepts in the SKOS hierarchy.

Decision Analysis
A structured methodology for evaluating alternatives under uncertainty using utility theory, probability assessment, and value-focused thinking. Produces a defensible, documented recommendation. Applied in: SE trade studies, project selection, risk management.
Behavioral Decision Theory
Empirical study of how humans actually make decisions — including cognitive biases, heuristics, framing effects, and loss aversion — diverging systematically from rational-actor models. Critical for requirements elicitation and human-system interface design.
Game Theory
Mathematical study of strategic interaction among rational agents whose outcomes depend on the choices of others. Applied in: multi-stakeholder negotiation, adversarial system design, mechanism design for incentive alignment.
Operations Research
Quantitative methods — linear programming, queuing theory, simulation, network optimization — for optimizing resource allocation, scheduling, and routing under constraints. Applied in: logistics, capacity planning, mission scheduling.
Bayesian Inference
A probabilistic framework for updating beliefs in light of evidence, using Bayes' theorem to combine prior knowledge with observed data. The formal basis for rational belief revision under uncertainty. Applied in: sensor fusion, reliability estimation, AI reasoning.
Multi-Criteria Decision Analysis (MCDA)
A framework for evaluating alternatives against multiple, potentially conflicting criteria using weighted scoring, outranking, or value functions. Applied in: system selection, architecture trade studies, strategic prioritization. Methods: AHP, TOPSIS, ELECTRE, MAVT.
Scenario Planning
A structured method for exploring multiple plausible futures by developing distinct narrative scenarios and testing the robustness of decisions against each. Not probabilistic — explores the space of possibilities. Applied in: strategic planning, ConOps development, wargaming.
Risk Analysis
Systematic identification, assessment, and prioritization of risks — probability-weighted consequences of hazards — followed by application of mitigation strategies. The intersection of Decision Science and Safety Engineering. Applied in: STPA, FMEA, program risk management.
