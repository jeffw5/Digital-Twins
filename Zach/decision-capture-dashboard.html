Decision capture in the agentic loop

Working model for the Welz and Walk collaboration. Draft, September 2026.

A decision passes through five stages. A human holds intent and approves what crosses a threshold; an agent does the work; a second agent challenges it; a memory substrate holds the record. Tap a stage to see who acts and what gets written.

Why capture happens here

A person moving through consult, reason, reframe, delegate, escalate, and commit leaves no trace unless asked to. An agent in a harness makes those moves as actual tool calls and prompts, so the traversal record is a side effect of running the work. The human's own decisions are captured at the two points where the harness has to stop and ask (framing and approval), which is where the prediction and its failure condition are also forced.

The one step that blocks on data the agent cannot get from the substrate is the pre-commit prediction. In every other digital environment that step is an authoring task nobody performs; here it is a form the agent cannot skip.

Four parties, six channels. The substrate is Utraya (an RDF baseline of assertions with provenance and two time axes) with holonic containment supplying the scoped views. Tap a channel for what crosses it.

C1 Human to executor. Intent, budgets, approval.

Inbound to the agent: the task, the framing question if the human states one, and budgets on the constrained qualities (cost, time, acceptable risk). Outbound to the human: the framed decision for confirmation, and answers to questions the human marks critical.

Writes: a DecisionRecord is opened with the human as authority; each Budget carries the human as owner. In Jeff's terms the budgets are where the guardrail gate lives, as an ordered constraint set rather than a fixed ethic.

C2 Adversary to human. Escalation.

The adversary estimates the value and impact of each decision and passes the ones above threshold to the human with the frontier and its own challenge attached. The executor never estimates its own escalations, since an executor that decides what reaches its overseer controls its own oversight. Below threshold the adversary clears the decision back to the executor.

Writes: a TraceEvent with move escalate, the escalatedTo link, and the status change. Jeff's circuit breaker levels (L1 alert, L2 hold, L3 halt) are the threshold policy this channel enforces for AI-in-the-loop alternatives.

C3 Executor and adversary. Challenge.

The executor hands over the frontier and its intended choice. The adversary returns a challenge in one of three modes (stress the plan for exploitable failure, force the executor to defend or adapt, introduce a different framing) together with any frontier options the executor neglected.

Writes: TraceEvents with move reframe or reason, additional Alternative entries, and any divergence found between an option's declared and observed profile.

C4 Executor and substrate. Scoped view and assertions.

Query: a task-scoped projection of the baseline, built by the executor's role holon (interior graph, boundary, projection, context) with its own weights over entries. The interface is a budgeted query simple enough for the least capable worker, because a structure that can only be reached through sophisticated queries has relocated the demand for capability rather than reduced it.

Write: every assertion the executor makes (alternatives, qualities, the prediction, the commit) lands as an assertion with the executor as asserting agent, a valid-time interval, and a transaction time. Portals validate with SHACL and record provenance on traversal. All operations are reachable over MCP.

C5 Adversary and substrate. Independent view.

The adversary holds its own projection with its own weights and is never provisioned by the executor, since a critic provisioned by its target is a compliance exercise. Its standing queries cover neglected frontier options and declared-versus-observed divergence.

Write: value and impact estimates, divergence findings, and its own TraceEvents, each attributed to the adversary as asserting agent.

C6 Measurement to substrate. Outcomes and reinforcement.

Telemetry, test results, twin runs, or cost and schedule actuals arrive against the MeasurementSignal the prediction named. The outcome is scored against the prediction and its falsifier; the gap reinforces or attenuates the pattern that was used.

Write: Outcome, observed Quality values, the gap, and a Pattern lifecycle event (reinforce or attenuate). Attenuation without reinforcement retires the pattern in time, so stale structure leaves without an auditor finding it.

Alignment to PROV-O

A DecisionRecord is a prov:Activity. Each Alternative, Prediction, Outcome, Requirement, and TestCase is a prov:Entity; a requirement wasDerivedFrom the decision that produced it. An Actor is a prov:Agent with a role qualifier. A TraceEvent is a qualified association whose role is the move, which is what makes the traversal policy a query over many records rather than a story told after the fact.

The prototype records its own construction

Decision records mined from an existing program's artifacts inherit whatever that program failed to write down, so they are a poor test of the schema. The prototype instead runs the loop on a decision process the two collaborators control: the building of the prototype itself. Every decision taken while building it (schema choices, stack choices, scope cuts, what to test) passes through the harness and lands in the store, and the record is tied to the software artifacts the decision produced.

The trace chain is decision to requirement to test case to result. A decision derives or constrains one or more requirements; a requirement is verified by one or more test cases; a test run is the measurement. The prediction at commit names the tests expected to pass and the falsifier names the tests whose failure would mean the decision was wrong, so continuous integration closes the loop without a separate measurement system. This is Fitch's decision-to-requirements traceability with the requirement written as a software requirement and the verification written as a test.

Prototype plan

Zach

  1. Write the decision record schema in specl, extended with Requirement and TestCase, and run it through the review tool.
  2. Set up the harness so every decision in building the prototype is taken through the loop (frame, consult, weigh, commit with prediction and falsifier) and written to the store.
  3. Keep the prototype's own requirements and tests in the store, linked from the decisions that produced them, with CI results written back as outcomes.
  4. Render the store in g3-toolkit as a breakdown structure with drill-down, frontier toggle, and trace from decision through requirement to test result.
  5. Report on the record after the first working increment: which decisions were escalated, which predictions failed, which patterns were promoted.

Jeff

  1. Define the decision classes and framing questions the software effort will use, drawn from the Fitch corpus, so the records are typed from the first commit.
  2. Author the Causal Impact Map template for a software decision (strategy, service, process, asset) with a named test or metric as the measurement signal.
  3. Recast the guardrail gate as a configurable constraint set and state the thresholds the prototype's own decisions are held to.
  4. Specify which prototype decisions carry a circuit breaker record (those that hand a choice to a predicting agent) and what the L1 to L3 bands are.
  5. Own the narrative and the customer conversation, using the prototype's own record as the demonstration.

Together

  1. Agree the schema first; the first decision written to the store is the decision to adopt it.
  2. Build the smallest increment that can record a decision, derive a requirement, run a test, and score the outcome, and take every decision along the way through the loop.
  3. Review the record together after that increment before adding federation, a second decision class, or a customer's process.
  4. Publish the schema and the record of the build as a joint paper, with the tooling following as the commercial artifact.

Open points for discussion

  • The lexicographic gate needs thresholds and a treatment of incomparability; without them the judgment that an alternative degrades a boundary is made silently, which defeats the traceability aim.
  • The circuit breaker instruments only alternatives that hand a live decision to a predicting agent. The declared-versus-observed audit applies to partners and non-AI alternatives as well, and the record should carry it for both.
  • Five strata plus twins plus ecosystem is a platform pitch. The record and its traceability are the sellable unit; federation and twins stay on the roadmap.
  • Whether Jeff's H·O·V·A quad-tuple (holonic boundary, domain ontology, vector context, domain agent) is a role holon in the substrate's sense, or a separate governance object that references one, is unsettled and decides how the circuit breaker record is stored.
  • A self-recorded build is a favorable test of capture and an unfavorable test of generality; the record of the build should say which decisions would not have been captured under a customer's process.
