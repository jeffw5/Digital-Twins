Spine

Four claims everything else rests on

Everything below follows from one of these. I have written them as claims because I want them argued with.

1 · The substrate should be RDF, and RDF is already a hypergraphNamed graphs make graphs simultaneously containers and participants. Once you accept that, the RDF-vs-LPG debate dissolves into a tooling problem and the LPG becomes a validated projection inside the RDF system.
2 · Composition needs a governed unitNaive graph merge fails in production. The holon gives you four graphs: interior (what is true), boundary (what is permitted), projection (what outsiders see), context (where this belongs). That is enough structure to govern a federation instead of just describing one.
3 · Validation is the contract, not the schemaSHACL shapes are executable semantic contracts at every boundary. Which is what lets you integrate as-is sources incrementally instead of waiting on a comprehensive enterprise ontology that never lands.
4 · Intelligence lives in the traversal policyNeither the graph alone nor the model alone accounts for it. If that is right, the policy can be externalized, instrumented, logged, and compared, and structure may substitute for parameter scale. That prediction is falsifiable, and I wrote the experiment down before running it.

Claim 4 is the newest and the least defended. I will argue 1 through 3 confidently. For 4 I want data.

Shipping

Software — three projects, three layers

All three are installable and public. They stack: specl governs how things get specified, holonic is the graph substrate, g3-toolkit is how a human sees it. None of them is finished.

v0.7.x → 0.8 · MIT · PyPI holonic — holonic RDF graphs Python client for building holonic knowledge graphs on Cagle's four-graph model. Every holon is an IRI whose named graphs live in a quad store — "the dataset IS the holarchy; Python methods are convenience, not architecture." Portals are RDF triples in boundary graphs, discovered by SPARQL and traversed by executing CONSTRUCT queries. Membrane validation runs SHACL over interior∪boundary. Ships an OWL 2 RL cga: vocabulary, rdflib and Fuseki backends behind a pluggable store protocol, and 13 example notebooks (12 run in-browser on JupyterLite).
v1.0.0-rc.2 · Apache 2.0 · npm g3-toolkit — graph visualization components TypeScript/React monorepo: @g3t/core (Universal Graph Model over graphology, adapters for SPARQL/Cypher/Gremlin/REST, RDF→LPG projection pipeline, SHACL validation, diffing, undo/redo), @g3t/react (12 views incl. Cytoscape canvas, timeline, map, schema, SHACL browser, diff, matrix, Sankey; 15 controls), @g3t/charts (linked ECharts with two-way selection). It is a component library by design; the application shell is left to you. README code blocks typecheck in CI "so it cannot rot silently."
early · MIT · PyPI specl — RDF-native, SHACL-validated specifications "The durable source of truth that AI coding agents read before they write." Markdown spec → Turtle → SHACL validation, scoring, diffing, badges. Requirements carry stable IDs (R1.2, US3) so they diff mechanically. Two-tier severity: structural violations always fail; production-readiness warnings only gate once a spec declares specl:status "production", so CI doesn't get rewired as a spec matures. Permanent w3id.org hash namespaces. It specifies itself.

Open experiment

Structure Substitutes for Scale — decision policy as the engineered object

The object this paper is about is a traversal policy: the decision logic at each node of a problem-solving process. Retrieve or reason. Reframe or persist. Delegate or dig deeper. Stop or continue. Skill is the tuning of that logic, and no tuning dominates all problems. If the difference between good and poor problem solvers is policy rather than hardware, then the policy can be externalized, instrumented, compared, and improved. Which makes this decision-support work before it is AI work.

It started from a narrow observation: agentic systems currently acquire tools by injection. Every connected capability is serialized into context, where it competes with the task for attention. Tool knowledge belongs in a governable structure outside the model, queried through a deliberately simple interface.

1 · Decision rights, and who gets to estimate value
HumanAuthority and manager. Owns intent, sets direction, approves above a value-and-impact threshold, fields questions the system judges critical.
→
OrchestratorDecomposes work and provisions capability by modulating edge weights over the tool graph, passing each subagent a projection containing only what its task requires.
→
AdversaryMalicious / contrarian / perspectival. Holds its own traversal weights and a direct escalation channel to the human.
The design creates a principal-agent problem and then has to answer for it. Someone has to estimate the value and impact of a decision in order to determine what reaches the human for approval, and an orchestrator that self-estimates controls its own oversight. The adversary therefore doubles as an escalation auditor, empowered to tattle. Its two independence requirements follow from the same reasoning: separate traversal weights over the shared graph, because an orchestrator that provisions its own critic can starve it, and an escalation path that bypasses the orchestrator entirely. "A red team provisioned by its target is a compliance exercise."
2 · Decision tiering, and where it meets your multi-tier architecture
The threshold is the open questionMy paper posits a value-and-impact threshold governing what escalates to the human, and then admits it does not say how to set it. Your Tier 1 / 2 / 3 decomposition (operational seconds-to-minutes, tactical hours-to-days, strategic weeks-to-quarters, each engaging different holonic levels) is a far more developed answer than anything in my draft.
What I add to itTiering tells you what should escalate. It does not tell you whether escalation is happening honestly. The adversary is the auditor for the tiering itself — the component that notices when things stop being escalated, which is the failure mode a tier definition cannot catch on its own.
Deontic norms are boundary graphsYour LKIF obligation / permission / prohibition / violation / sanction library and my boundary graph ("what is permitted") are the same construct at different levels of formality. SHACL is one way to make the permission layer executable; a violation is a shape failure and a sanction is what the portal does about it. A clean, small integration point.
Diversity as a decision assetEffectiveness is always relative to a subset of problems, so a population's diversity of policies is a feature to preserve rather than noise to average away. Applied to decision support, this argues against convergence on a single approved method — the thing most governance programs drift toward by default.
3 · The decision audit trail

Decision nodes (query, ruminate, reframe, delegate, stop) are part of the design's logging requirements from the start, on the same footing as the results. The reasoning is an evidence bar the paper sets against itself:

A traversal policy that cannot be observed is an introspective story, and introspective stories are cheap.

This is also the input your human-factors instrumentation needs and does not currently have a source for. Trust calibration — acceptance rate against actual accuracy, bucketed by stated confidence — is not computable without a per-decision record of what was proposed, what was accepted, and what turned out to be true. The holonic provenance layer already records traversals and validations, which covers most of it.

4 · Multi-criteria structure — dominance now, scalarization at decision time

The rule

  • Where functionally equivalent capabilities differ on competing performance qualities, pruning resolves into dominance.
  • Dominated options can be down-weighted without knowing the task.
  • The non-dominated set must be preserved.
  • Scalarizing the frontier becomes task-time provisioning work rather than graph-time curation.
  • Exploring neglected non-dominated alternatives is a standing assignment for the adversary.

Why it matters for decision support

  • A curated catalog that scalarizes early has already made the tradeoff on the decision-maker's behalf, and made it without knowing what the decision was.
  • Preserving the frontier keeps the tradeoff where the context lives. The weights arrive with the problem.
  • This is the same move as separating rule definition from rule execution in your compiled governance, applied to options instead of constraints.
  • It also gives "measuring the right value" a mechanism: you cannot measure the right value if the alternatives were collapsed into one before anyone stated an objective.
5 · Declared versus enacted — evidence bounds on capability

A tool's declared semantics (its self-description) and its enacted semantics (what the provenance corpus shows it doing) deserve distinct edge types, because their divergence is itself the signal. Auditing that divergence is another standing assignment for the adversary.

Verifiable lower boundBehaviors witnessed in the provenance record
Unverifiable upper boundBehaviors that cannot be ruled out
ConsequenceOpacity makes enacted intent unprovable, not uninterpretable. Schema structure, provenance, and relational context all carry evidence.

For a decision process this is the gap between the documented procedure and the observed one, expressed so that it can be queried rather than discovered during an incident review.

6 · The prediction, written down before running it

Graph-mediated provisioning should flatten the performance gap between frontier-scale and sub-billion-parameter models. Measurement is decomposed into three layers so the effect can be located, not just detected:

Layer 1Tool discovery & selection accuracy — where equalization is predicted to appear most strongly
→
Layer 2Argument construction validity
→
Layer 3End-to-end task success — partial effect predicted; a small model may select correctly and still fail multi-step execution
Confound: context capacityA reduced tool subset that fits the small model's window, separating "cannot fit" from "cannot reason over what fits." Both outcomes support the thesis by different mechanisms, and conflating them would be cheating.
Confound: curation fairnessThe in-context baseline gets equivalently well-organized descriptions, so the experiment measures structure and not effort.
Confound: instrumentation parityDecision nodes logged identically across all conditions, so traversal policies stay observable in every arm.

Why the result matters for decision support specifically. If structure substitutes for scale, governed decision support runs on models small enough to sit inside a sovereign boundary, at a cost and latency that make per-decision instrumentation affordable. The whole human-factors program — calibration tracking, confidence decomposition, workload matching — depends on being able to instrument every decision rather than a sampled few. Right now that is priced out at frontier-model rates. If the prediction holds, it stops being.

Falsification conditions are stated in advance. Harness is private during development and will be released with results in a companion paper. Status: preregistered, not yet run.

7 · The honest gap, and the best single place to collaborate

holonic is the candidate realization: an MCP server is a holon, its interior holds full schemas, its boundary is where provisioning writes edge weights, its projections are literally what a subagent sees, its context holds registry membership, and portals give crystallized handlers a first-class primitive. But:

The four layers exist structurally, and there is no dynamics layer. Mutations are event-triggered rather than driven by a learning signal, and validation shapes validate rather than transform. The gap between a mutation surface, which exists (nine discrete operations over holons, layers, and portals), and a mutation policy, which does not, is the fundamental research I am proposing.

This is also where Holonic Neural Networks failed. The negative result and the open problem turn out to be the same thing, which is why the postmortem was worth writing.

Societal scale

Viral engineering — when the swarm is society

Project AEGIS treats agent-swarm safety as epidemiology inside an enterprise, where you own both the execution plane and the control plane. The harder version of the problem is the same dynamics with no org chart: outputs from large-scale AI propagating through populations, institutions, and training corpora, where no single party has authority over the whole system and most participants have not agreed to be governed. I think the substrate work above bears more directly on that version than on the enterprise one. Here is the argument, and then its limits.

Societal failure modeWhy it is a graph problemWhat I have that bears on it
Propagation with no traceable originAn AI output enters a document, gets cited, gets scraped, re-enters a model, and returns as apparent consensus. By then nobody can say where it started or who else is carrying it. Over unmarked text, retraction is not a defined operation at all. Over a graph of typed, addressable, provenance-bearing assertions it is, because you can query what depends on the claim you just pulled. The digital thread paper's central cut — atomic assertion, connected graph, and the machinery that merely realizes them — is what makes a coverage or retraction claim mean something. PROV-O provenance is already in holonic. Limit: this needs marking at authoring time. Retrofitting provenance onto an unmarked corpus is unsolved and I have no route to it.
Corpus contaminationMachine output re-entering training data. Your poster already flags anti-collapse at the enterprise boundary; the marking has to survive leaving the enterprise, which is where it currently stops. Named graphs let origin be asserted about content rather than embedded in it, and asserted by parties other than the author. Under the hypergraph reading, a claim about a corpus is itself a first-class graph: queryable, contestable, and attributable to whoever made it. The RDF-as-hypergraph position plus named-graph provenance. Limit: an assertion layer only works if institutions maintain it. That maintenance burden is organizational, and no format carries it for you.
Correlated failure from policy monocultureIf every institution's decision support runs the same few frontier models under the same traversal policy, their errors correlate. A population that shares a policy fails together and fails quietly, because each participant's confidence is reinforced by everyone else agreeing. You cannot preserve diversity the system has no way to represent. The dominance rule — down-weight dominated options, preserve the non-dominated set, scalarize at decision time — is a systemic-risk control expressed as a graph policy rather than as a regulation. The problem itself is well established. Kleinberg & Raghavan (PNAS 2021) show convergence on a common algorithm can lower the collective quality of decisions even with no shock and no correlated failure; Bommasani et al. (NeurIPS 2022) find that shared components homogenize outcomes without degrading measured accuracy, so the correlation grows behind stable metrics. What I add is a different sharing surface — the traversal policy sitting above the model, which is not what those results measure — and an economic mechanism in place of a mandate: structure substituting for scale is what makes diversity affordable. If small models with good structure work, many differently-tuned systems become viable instead of everyone renting the same three. Limit: strongest claim on this page, resting entirely on an experiment I have not run.
Institutional complacencyYour Week-52 table has an operator drifting to 70% oversight while reliability falls to 85% and the AI keeps improving. The same curve runs above the operator: a reviewer, an auditor, or a regulator that comes to rely on AI-generated evidence rubber-stamps at institutional scale, and the drift stays invisible because the evidence looks better than what it replaced. Calibration at any scale needs a decision record: what was proposed, at what stated confidence, what was accepted, what turned out to be true. Above the individual, that record has to cross organizational boundaries, which makes it a federation problem as much as a statistics one. The decision-node log from the zone above, and the federated interchange pattern for moving calibration evidence across a boundary without surrendering the underlying case data. Limit: I have no human-subjects work. Your framework has the constructs and the thresholds. This half is yours and I would be the one learning.
Oversight captureAn auditor whose data access is granted by the audited party is structurally compromised regardless of anyone's intent. Boundary graphs make "who may see what, granted by whom, revocable by whom" an inspectable artifact rather than a paragraph in a charter. "Could this overseer have been starved?" becomes a query with an answer. The adversary's two independence requirements are institutional design principles that happen to be expressible in a graph. Limit: expressing a property does not enforce it. Enforcement here is legal and organizational; the graph only makes the violation visible.
Containment across parties who will not share dataAEGIS assumes you own both planes. Society-scale containment involves counterparties outside your org chart, some adversarial, most unwilling to hand over a corpus or their telemetry. SHACL shapes let a counterparty validate fitness-for-purpose without access to the underlying data. The shape travels and the data stays put, which is a public-health-style shared protocol with no central data authority. The federated zero-trust interchange architecture: MCP as the discovery and negotiation control plane, SHACL as executable contract at every boundary. Limit: draft-stage paper with no implementation behind it.
Self-modification with no governed mutation policyA system with a mutation surface and no policy over that surface is the uncontrolled case by definition. The mutation surface in holonic is nine discrete operations over holons, layers, and portals. Discreteness is the point: a structural change you can name is one you can gate, log, and reverse. I built one of these and it failed, and the failure is published with a DOI. That is an argument for doing the mutation-policy research and a caution against putting structural self-modification into anything socially consequential before the policy exists.
Your AEGIS metrics, computed on this substrate
Fan-out rate — RfDistinct downstream agents reached per originating call, counted over the provenance graph
Delegation loop density — DlCycle detection over delegation edges recorded in context graphs
Token amplification — AtRatio of emitted to ingested volume along a traversal path

All three are topological measures over a record of who invoked what. If traversals are recorded as RDF rather than as log lines, they become SPARQL queries over context graphs instead of a separate instrumentation build. This is the fastest thing to build on this page, and it depends on none of my unproven claims.

What I am not claiming
  • Nothing here is validated at population scale. The largest thing I have actually run is a lab implementation that broke.
  • The epidemiological framing is borrowed, not derived. Whether Rf behaves like a reproduction number — whether it has a threshold, whether interventions move it predictably — is an empirical question I have not seen anyone answer.
  • The human-factors claims are extensions of your framework, not results of mine. Fatigue scoring, trust calibration, competency decay, and production-pressure detection are your constructs; I am pointing at where a graph substrate would feed them, and no further.
  • Provenance-based control only constrains participants. It does nothing about parties outside the scheme, which today is most of the internet. Anyone who tells you a metadata standard fixes societal AI harm is selling something.
  • The diversity argument depends on the unrun experiment. If structure does not substitute for scale, the affordability claim collapses and monoculture is simply what the economics buy.
The split is clean enough to state plainly: you have the human factors, the organizational design, and a deployment surface. I have the substrate, the provenance model, and one falsifiable claim about whether policy diversity is affordable. Neither half reaches the societal version of this problem alone.

Aggregate trust scoresYou already say "dimensional, never aggregate" for the trust metric, which I agree with. I'd extend it: a tool's declared semantics and its enacted semantics (what the provenance corpus shows it doing) deserve distinct edge types, because the divergence is itself the signal — with a verifiable lower bound (behaviors witnessed) and an unverifiable upper bound (behaviors not ruled out).
What I'd bring that isn't in your topology at all
  • Running code. Three public repos, one on PyPI at 0.7.x, one at RC with CI-verified docs. Your reference architecture currently has no named implementation.
  • A published negative result. The HNN postmortem is a DOI-minted account of a structural-learning architecture that didn't work, with the failure modes documented. Approaches that did not work are expensive to learn and almost never written up.
  • A preregistered, falsifiable experiment with confound controls and stated failure conditions. It makes a different kind of claim than a reference topology does, and one that holds up under review.
  • Cross-organizational sovereignty. Your governance is intra-enterprise. The MCP + SHACL zero-trust interchange work covers the case where the counterparty is not in your org chart and never will be.
  • Spec discipline as tooling. specl makes requirement IDs, acceptance criteria, and status gates machine-checkable — directly usable for your KGCL change governance and your ARB packets.

Concrete places to start

Ordered by how quickly they'd produce something jointly citable.

  1. Compute Rf, Dl, and At as SPARQL over a provenance graph. Your AEGIS metrics are topological measures over a record of who invoked what. Record traversals as RDF and they stop being a separate instrumentation build. Days of work, no unproven claims involved, and it produces a figure.
  2. A joint position piece on monoculture as systemic risk. The problem is already documented from two directions: Kleinberg & Raghavan (PNAS 2021) and Bommasani et al. (NeurIPS 2022) on the algorithmic side, and the FSB's 2024 financial-stability assessment, which names market correlation and provider concentration among its four AI vulnerability categories. Two gaps are open. The FSB's own 2025 monitoring report concedes that market correlations are close to untrackable with current instrumentation, which is a measurement problem a decision-log substrate speaks to directly. And the standing interventions are regulatory — mandate pluralization, cap outcome correlation — where the argument I want to make is economic: make diversity cheap enough that it does not need mandating. You have the organizational and human-factors half. This is the one I think matters most, and the one I most want you to try to break.
  3. Run the experiment. Section 6 of Structure Substitutes for Scale is preregistered and unexecuted. Frontier model over API, small model local, three metric layers, three confound controls. A second party on the harness would strengthen the result, and the paper's own evidence bar asks for an independent hand on the instrumentation.
  4. The mutation policy. The mutation surface exists in holonic (nine discrete operations over holons, layers, and portals). The policy — what drives mutation from a learning signal, and how discrete structure co-evolves with a continuous representation — does not exist, in my work or yours. This is the actual research.
  5. Formalize a portal typology from your six correspondence types. Small, well-scoped, useful to both of us, and it ends up in a spec instead of a slide.
  6. Wire g3-toolkit into an L6 console against a real governance dataset. Fastest path to a demo either of us can show.
  7. Co-author on the DEIX side. The digital thread note and the twin artifact/role paper are both aimed at working-group consumption; the MBSE critique set (five drafts) needs a second opinion before it goes out, and one of them needs a citation audit I'd rather not do alone.
