Compiled semantic governance — SHACL/SWRL as single source of truth (Figure 6)

Four governance domains

"This is compiled semantic governance — rules are always defined once, in the graph layer, and harmonized centrally, but they are read, compiled, and enforced by every subsystem in this topology, never re-authored locally."

Virtualization layer (Figure 15) — context engineering & semantic optimization

Context engineering

  • Requirement context bundle (RCB) assembly — WHO/WHAT/WHEN/WHERE/WHY relevancy dimensions
  • Persona & session context binding — expiration, permission scope
  • Compiled AI directive assembly — governance outputs synthesized into a bounded content

Semantic optimization for execution

  • Pre-compiled rule projections — dbt/SQL/OPA/Rego, vector filters
  • Tiered materialization — pre-validated sets vs. federated live queries
  • Query virtualization & embedding / index caching

Runtime delivery — microsecond-to-second latency, no graph reasoning at request time, MTBH-style trust score attached to every response. Consumed by the L3 gateway facade, L6 orchestration & UI, and L7 enterprise ecosystem.

Trust — human side

Human-AI teaming framework

"We've built trustworthy AI through rigorous technical safeguards. This framework extends those same principles to the people who work with it." A parallel layer added to the semantic governance architecture, the AI circuit breaker, and the Tom Sawyer OPS Workbench — operationalizing the abstract HITL governance spine (Figure 16) above with real-time instrumentation of the humans in the loop.

The automation paradox — why this exists
MilestoneAI accuracyHuman oversightSystem reliability
Week 165%95%95%
Week 52 — without this framework92%70% (rubber-stamping)85% — worse, despite better AI
Week 52 — with this framework92%94%99% — better AI, better system

Better automation alone makes human supervisors complacent — a pattern documented from Air France 447 to Three Mile Island to Knight Capital. Circuit breakers only shift the failure point unless the human who responds to a trip is also monitored, calibrated, and rested.

Seven integrated capabilities
Real-time performance monitoringFatigue score = 30% session duration + 30% time-since-break + 20% accuracy degradation + 20% decision-speed increase. Mandatory 15-min break at 0.7; 6-hour session cap
Trust calibrationTracks acceptance-rate vs. actual-accuracy per confidence bucket. <5% gap = calibrated · 5–15% = slight · >15% = over/under-trust, triggers a personalized in-context warning
Confidence explainabilityDecomposes each AI score into string similarity, semantic similarity, structural alignment, historical precedent, domain usage — plus evidence, concerns, and an interactive validation checklist
Cognitive workload management0–1 cognitive capacity score matches task complexity to engineer state; no task assigned below capacity floor — take a break instead
Competency maintenanceUnassisted mapping exercise every 14 days (60% correctness + 40% reasoning quality); knowledge quizzes every 30 days; conflict-resolution sims every 21 days; skill-degradation flag at −15% of baseline
Production pressure detectionIndividual + team pressure score from rushing, accuracy decline, alert overrides, burnout risk, off-hours work. >0.7 = critical — halts non-critical work, cuts targets 30–50%, forces time off
Enhanced interfacesTrust-warning banners, confidence breakdowns, interactive validation checklists, and Tom Sawyer Workbench human-performance dashboards alongside the technical metrics
