🧬 What "genomic sequencing" means here

Every agent's system prompt is vectorized at deploy time into a Reference Vector (Vref) — its "healthy genome." Outbound payloads are vectorized in real time into a Sample Vector (Vsample). Cosine divergence between the two, plus structural "phenotypic markers" (role evasion, urgency/coercion, recursive looping), produce a single Semantic Drift Score (Δ) — the agentic equivalent of a PCR test. See the Drift Score Calculator tab to try it live.

🧭 How the Immune Response Is Orchestrated

Real immune systems run two coordination models at once — innate immunity (fast, local, non-specific: neutrophils act on-site within minutes) and adaptive immunity (slower, systemic, precise: T-cells and antibodies get coordinated centrally once a threat is confirmed). This platform mirrors both. Toggle between them below.

Levels 1–2: a cluster's own OPA sidecars detect drift, synthesize a Rego "vaccine" locally, and distribute it only within that cluster/role group. Neighboring clusters may subscribe to the new antibody signature peer-to-peer, but nothing requires them to. Fast (sub-second) and resilient — no single outage takes down containment fleet-wide — but coverage can be inconsistent across clusters that haven't seen the signature yet.

Level 3: once a mutated agent breaches a cloud boundary, containment can no longer be left to independent clusters — a central authority (CERT-equivalent, typically with human SOC sign-off) computes a single Semantic Antibody payload and broadcasts hard circuit-breaker commands to every federated trust domain simultaneously. Slower to trigger (requires confirmation) but guarantees consistent, systemic containment — the trade-off innate/federated response can't offer.
